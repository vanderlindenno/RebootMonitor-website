Shared devices deserve regular reboots

Shared Android devices are used by many people and owned by no one, so they almost never get restarted. RebootMonitor makes uptime visible and nudges the current user to reboot.

The shared device problem

In healthcare, education, logistics and field service, one device is often shared by a whole team.

A nurse on the day shift uses it in the morning and a colleague on the evening shift picks it up later. Nobody feels responsible for the device, so nobody thinks of restarting it. Weeks or even months without a reboot are the norm rather than the exception.

This page explains how shared devices are set up with Microsoft Intune and Managed Home Screen, why a missing reboot causes problems, and how RebootMonitor closes that gap.

Intune and Managed Home Screen

Intune enrolls a shared device as an Android Enterprise dedicated device. Managed Home Screen decides what the user sees on it.

Managed Home Screen (MHS) is a Microsoft launcher that replaces the standard home screen. Users only see the apps the administrator has approved and cannot change device settings. A shared device stays predictable, no matter who is holding it.

MHS can also require a Microsoft Entra ID sign-in per session and sign users out automatically after inactivity. That makes the device suitable for changing users. It also makes the device feel like it belongs to no one.

Why a reboot matters

A device that runs for weeks can fall behind on updates and become slower and less reliable.

Pending updates

Android system updates and many security patches only take effect after a restart. The device can report as compliant while still running old code.

Memory & performance

Background processes pile up over time. Apps slow down or crash, and the battery drains faster.

Policies & apps

New Intune profiles, app updates and MHS configuration are sometimes only fully applied after a restart.

Helpdesk tickets

Many "the device is acting strange" reports disappear after a reboot. Without one, they end up at the service desk.

No owner

An owner notices and restarts a personal device. On a shared device, everyone passes the problem to the next user.

What happens when nobody owns the device?

Everyone uses the device. Nobody thinks about restarting it.

  • MondayEmployee A uses the device for a full shift. No restart.
  • TuesdayEmployee B takes over. It still works, so there is no reason to restart it.
  • WednesdayEmployee C uses it. Again, no restart.
  • ThursdayThe device has now been running for several days. Nobody necessarily notices.
  • FridayIt still works, so it keeps being used. The cycle can go on indefinitely.

The problem is not that users are doing something wrong. A shared device has no natural owner, so the question of who restarts it is easily overlooked.

The solution: RebootMonitor

RebootMonitor records every reboot locally and reminds the user once the device has gone without a restart for too long — 7 days by default, adjustable to 3 or 14 days, or off.

The app speaks to the person holding the device right now. They can see the device's status at a glance and get a clear reason to restart it.

How it gets users to reboot

  • After the chosen interval without a reboot (7 days by default, or 3 or 14 days), a notification appears and the app icon gets a badge.
  • Opening the app shows a pop-up that recommends restarting the device.
  • The reminder clears itself after the next reboot. It stays silent for the first reminder period after installation, and can be turned off entirely.
  • If a reminder doesn't arrive as expected on a particular device, a built-in shortcut jumps straight to its battery optimization settings.

Private and simple

  • All data stays on the device in a local database. Nothing is sent anywhere.
  • Only two permissions: RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED and POST_NOTIFICATIONS.
  • History older than 12 months is cleaned up automatically. The app follows the device language, in 10 languages.

Deploying with Intune

Deploy RebootMonitor like any other Managed Google Play app. The one extra step is adding its icon to Managed Home Screen.

  1. Add the app

    In the Intune admin center, go to Apps › Android › Add › Managed Google Play app and approve RebootMonitor. Package name: app.rebootmonitor.mobile.

  2. Assign it

    Assign the app as Required to the device group that holds the shared devices.

  3. Add it to MHS

    Add the app to the allowed apps in the dedicated device profile (multi-app kiosk) or the MHS app configuration policy.

  4. Allow notifications

    Keep the notification bar available in MHS, so users actually see the reminder. On Android 13 and later, also let Intune grant the notification permission so users are not asked for it. See how below.

  5. Verify

    Open the app on a test device, check the uptime and reboot once. The reboot should appear in the overview right away.

  6. Set the reminder centrally (optional)

    An app configuration policy can set the reminder for every device and, with a license key, hide the settings. See the configuration section below.

  7. Check battery optimization if needed

    Most devices deliver the reminder fine on default battery settings. If a reminder doesn't show up on a particular device, users can check the shortcut in the app's settings (our step-by-step guide shows exactly what to tap), or, on Samsung devices, an admin can manage that setting fleet-wide through Intune using the Samsung Knox Service Plugin (OEMConfig) instead of relying on each device.

Without step 3 the app is installed but invisible. Reminders only start after the chosen interval (7 days by default) has passed since installation, so don't expect notifications right after the rollout.

Configure RebootMonitor from Intune

From version 1.21 an app configuration policy sets the reboot reminder on every device from one place. With a license key (from version 1.22) it can also hide the settings button and the website button, and show your organization's name in the app.

Everything is read on the device. The policy needs no extra permission and no network connection, and nothing leaves the device. Without a policy the app works exactly as before.

What the user sees

  • When the reminder is set by policy, the reminder choices in Settings show the value that applies but are greyed out, under the note “Managed by your organisation”. A setting you leave out gets the app default (on, 7 days).
  • The user's own choice stays saved and returns if you remove the policy. A change in Intune applies at once while the app is open, and otherwise the next time it is opened.
  • Without a valid license key, Hide settings and Hide website button do nothing: the buttons stay, and Settings says why. The reminder settings work either way.
  • With a valid license key the information screen shows your organization's name in place of the donation link.

Set it up in Intune

  1. Create the policy

    Go to Apps › App configuration policies › Add › Managed devices. Platform: Android Enterprise. Profile type: Fully Managed, Dedicated and Corporate-Owned Work Profile (for shared devices and kiosks), or Personal Work Profile.

  2. Pick the app

    Targeted app: RebootMonitor.

  3. Add the settings

    Choose Use configuration designer and add the settings you want, or choose Enter JSON data and paste the example below.

  4. Assign it

    Assign the policy to the same device group as the app.

{
  "kind": "androidenterprise#managedConfiguration",
  "productId": "app:app.rebootmonitor.mobile",
  "managedProperty": [
    { "key": "reminder_enabled", "valueBool": true },
    { "key": "reminder_days", "valueString": "7" },
    { "key": "license_key", "valueString": "RM1.…your license key…" },
    { "key": "hide_settings", "valueBool": true },
    { "key": "hide_website", "valueBool": true }
  ]
}

Menu names can differ a little between Intune versions. Leave out license_key, hide_settings and hide_website if you only want to set the reminder.

Let Intune allow notifications

The reminder is a notification. From Android 13 an app has to ask the user for permission to show notifications, and a user can say no. On managed devices you can grant that permission centrally with a device restrictions profile, not with the app configuration policy above. The user is then not asked, and a reminder cannot be missed because of a refused prompt.

  1. Create a profile

    Go to Devices › Configuration › Create. Platform: Android Enterprise. Profile type: Device restrictions, under Fully Managed, Dedicated and Corporate-Owned Work Profile (or Personal Work Profile).

  2. Auto grant permissions

    Under General, set Default permission policy to Auto grant. Android then grants the permissions an app asks for without showing the prompt, including notifications.

  3. Show system notifications

    Set System notifications and information to show them in the device's status bar. On a kiosk with Managed Home Screen, also keep the notification bar available there.

  4. Assign it

    Assign the profile to the same device group as the app, and test it on one device first.

Good to know. The permission is granted the first time the app asks for it, so open the app once after the profile arrives. A user who already refused notifications earlier is not overruled by this setting: reinstall the app or reset that device. Default permission policy applies to all apps in the work profile, not only RebootMonitor. Before Android 13 notifications are on by default and nothing is needed.

Tips for a lasting reboot habit

Technology alone won't do it. RebootMonitor works best when the organization supports the habit too.

Put it up front

Give the icon a fixed, visible spot in the MHS grid, so the badge stands out.

Make it routine

Keep it short

Combine with management

Frequently asked questions

Why do shared Android devices rarely get restarted?

Shared devices are used by changing people who do not feel responsible for the device. Managed Home Screen sign-in and automatic sign-out make it feel even less like anyone's own device, so nobody thinks of rebooting it.

How often should a shared Android device be rebooted?

At least once every 7 days. That lets pending Android system and security updates take effect and clears background processes that slow the device down.

Does RebootMonitor work with Microsoft Intune and Managed Home Screen?

Yes. Deploy it as a Managed Google Play app, assign it as Required to your shared device group and add it to the allowed apps in Managed Home Screen.

Does RebootMonitor send any data to a server?

No. All reboot history is stored locally on the device. The app only uses the RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED and POST_NOTIFICATIONS permissions.

When does the reboot reminder appear?

By default, when the device has not been restarted for 7 days. That interval can be changed to 3 or 14 days, or turned off, from the app's settings menu. The reminder clears itself after the next reboot and stays silent during the first reminder period after installation. A shortcut to the device's battery optimization settings is also included, in case a reminder ever doesn't arrive as expected.

Can battery optimization be managed centrally instead of per device?

On Samsung devices, yes. Deploy the Samsung Knox Service Plugin as an OEMConfig app through Intune and use its settings to exempt RebootMonitor from battery optimization across the whole fleet, instead of relying on the in-app shortcut on every device. This is especially useful when you hide the app's settings button with a policy, because users can then no longer open the in-app shortcut.

Can I set the reboot reminder from Intune?

Yes. From version 1.21 an app configuration policy can turn the reminder on or off and set it to 3, 7 or 14 days for every device. Users then see the value that applies, greyed out in Settings, under the note that their organization manages it. The settings are read on the device, so no extra permission or network connection is needed.

What does the license key do, and what stays free?

The reminder settings and the whole app are free. A license key is only needed to hide the settings button and the website button with an app configuration policy. A valid key also shows your organization's name in the information screen, in place of the donation link. Without a valid key the two hide settings do nothing and the buttons stay visible.

How do I get a license key, and what does it cost?

Use the contact form on the home page and tell us the name of your organization and roughly how many devices you manage. The price depends on the number of devices and is given on request. The key is one-time, has no end date and is checked offline in the app, so no data is sent anywhere. Paste it into the License key setting of the app configuration policy.

Can Intune allow notifications for RebootMonitor so users are not asked?

Yes, on Android 13 and later. In a device restrictions profile for Android Enterprise, set Default permission policy to Auto grant. Android then grants the notification permission the first time RebootMonitor asks for it, without a prompt. The same profile has a setting to show system notifications in the device's status bar. This is a separate profile from the app configuration policy, and the permission policy applies to all apps in the work profile. See the steps.

Does RebootMonitor replace Microsoft Intune?

No. RebootMonitor is not an MDM solution. Intune stays responsible for enrollment, configuration, apps, policies and management. RebootMonitor focuses only on reboot and uptime visibility.

Does RebootMonitor need a connection to Intune?

No. It is a normal Android app that tracks restarts on its own. It can be deployed alongside your other apps through your existing Android management setup, and it also works on devices outside an enterprise environment.

Can IT administrators see the reboot history remotely?

No. The history is stored locally on the device. RebootMonitor provides local visibility and reminders rather than a central cloud dashboard for all devices.

Will restarting the device fix every Android problem?

No. A restart can clear temporary processes and memory and can help with certain transient issues, but persistent problems should still be investigated through your normal IT support and device-management processes.

Is RebootMonitor only for companies?

No. Individual Android users can use it to see when their device was last restarted. The shared-device and Intune use case is simply where the usual sense of ownership is missing.
